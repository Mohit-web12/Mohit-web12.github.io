First year teacher budget

ContextIBM SkillsBuild micro internship with Teach For America, through Parker Dewey
ToolsExcel: inputs, take home pay, budget, charts, and a checks tab
ResultLiving alone: $38 short a month. With a roommate: $630 left over.

The question

Can a first year teacher in Philadelphia afford to live alone? The teacher in my model starts at $57,444, the 2026 to 2027 salary for a ten month teacher with a bachelor's degree. They're single, rent a one bedroom inside the city, take SEPTA instead of driving, and pay for a two year certification program out of pocket.

What I did

Gross to net. I worked down from the salary schedule one deduction at a time: the 8.25% pension contribution, 1.5% for medical, union dues, 7.65% FICA, federal tax with the 2026 brackets and standard deduction, 3.07% Pennsylvania tax, and Philadelphia's 3.735% wage tax. That leaves $3,190 a month, about two thirds of gross.

Costs, each with a source. Rent is Zumper's October 2026 average for a one bedroom, $1,414. Utilities come from PECO, PGW and water department rates, scaled down to a one bedroom. Food is the USDA low cost plan. Certification is the cheapest program on the partner list, $16,150 over two years, which works out to $687 a month with fees. Every input on the Inputs tab has its source and the date I last checked it.

Scenarios. Two changes, one at a time: split a two bedroom with a roommate, or teach 100 hours of summer school.

Checks. A separate tab runs 14 checks. Each one computes a number a second, independent way, like net pay rebuilt from every deduction listed separately, or the weekly food cost converted to monthly. All 14 pass. I used Claude to suggest categories and poke holes in my first draft, but I didn't take a number from it without opening the source myself. Twice its summary of a source was wrong, and I used the original figure.

What I found

Spending, including a $150 savings target, comes to $3,228, so living alone runs $38 short every month. A roommate in a two bedroom frees up about $668, leaving $630 over. Summer school adds about $258 a month after tax, leaving $220.

Paychecks make it tighter than the average suggests. The salary comes in 26 biweekly checks, so ten months get two checks ($2,944) and run about $284 short, and two months get three. On top of that, the teacher needs about $3,774 up front for the first month's rent, a deposit and basic setup.

Living alone works only with no loan payments and no surprises. Any student loan payment comes straight out of a budget that's already negative, and rent at Rentometer's $1,650 median would cost $236 more a month.

What I'd do next

  • Replace my weakest assumption, that a one bedroom uses about half the gas and water of a typical household, with real bills from listings.
  • Add a scenario with a student loan payment, since any payment comes straight out of the surplus.
  • Update the gas cost when 2026 numbers are out. The model uses PGW's 2025 average.
